let’s look back at that code when we concatenated a 0 bit at the end of our bus in the arithmetic operators video
```verilog
assign sum = {1'b0, x} + {1'b0, y};
```
now what exactly is `1'b0` , it turns out that this is a more explicit way to define numbers in Verilog, actually we can even go more explicit with our definition
so the standard way to define number in Verilog is as follows
$$
\text{[sign]} \text{[size]} \; '\; \text{[base]} \; [\text{value}]
$$
so if we looked back at our value `1'b0` here we didn’t define a sign since the sign is assumed by default to be positive and the zero don’t have a sign any way, then we have our size of one bit, and the base is `b` which means binary and finally our value is equal to $0$
